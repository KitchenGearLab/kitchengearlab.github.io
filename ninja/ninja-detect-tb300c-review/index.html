Is it right for your kitchen?

Choose Detect if automatic adjustment fits your blending routine. The meaningful comparison with BN751C is the control system and included containers; peak wattage does not prove a better smoothie.

A good fit if…

  • People who want automatic speed/time adjustment through BlendSense
  • Households alternating pitcher batches and one personal cup
  • Cooks willing to follow separate loading guidance for each container

Consider another format if…

  • You require a food-processor bowl
  • You want two personal cups in the standard documented bundle
  • You need a hot-liquid or carbonated-drink blender

Advantages and limits of the configuration

These are design trade-offs, not results from physical testing.

Reasons to consider it

  • BlendSense offers automatic adjustment rather than only a fixed timed program
  • Pitcher and cup support different batch routines
  • A dedicated cup can avoid using the full pitcher for one drink

Limits to account for

  • Adaptive controls do not guarantee a specific texture
  • One included cup may be limiting for multiple independent users
  • Several sharp components and containers need cleaning and storage

Automation is a workflow choice

BlendSense changes speed and time during the blending process. Consider whether that makes your routine easier than selecting a preset or manually controlling a blend. It is a control feature, not an independently measured result.

We have not compared smoothie texture, ingredient detection, noise or processing time with BN751C. If your recipes are predictable and you already know your preferred settings, the included cup count and accessory storage may matter more than automation.

Duo means pitcher and personal cup

The TB300C bundle documents one 24 oz cup alongside its 72 oz nominal pitcher. It should not be confused with a larger Detect system that includes additional processing accessories. Check the seller’s packing list instead of inferring contents from a product family photo.

Use the correct blade assembly and fill marks for each container. The Canadian owner guide prohibits hot and carbonated ingredients. Do not use the personal cup for dry grinding or treat a large nominal pitcher volume as a licence to overfill.

Separate peak power from ownership requirements

The 1700 W figure is a peak claim, while the rated electrical input is 1200 W at 120 V. Neither figure tells you how loud the blender is or how much energy a recipe uses. No quantified performance or efficiency comparison is made here.

Handle loose blades carefully and follow the guide for dishwasher loading, including the restriction on heated drying. Keep the base out of water. The benefit of two container formats comes with more parts to inspect, assemble, clean and store.

Questions before buying

Is TB300C a processor-and-blender kitchen system?

This Duo bundle documents a pitcher and personal cup, not a separate processor bowl.

Does it include two personal cups?

The cited TB300C bundle lists one 24 oz cup. Confirm the current seller packing list.

Can I blend hot or carbonated ingredients?

No. The Canadian owner guide prohibits hot and carbonated ingredients.
