Is it right for your kitchen?

Choose around the container workflow. If you want one removable heating unit and two sizes of dedicated glassware, the Crispi offers a different arrangement from a drawer air fryer. If you want two foods cooking independently at once, start with a dual-basket alternative instead.

A good fit if…

  • You want to serve or store food in dedicated glass cooking containers.
  • You alternate between a smaller portion and a larger batch, rather than cooking both independently at the same time.
  • You are comfortable following model-specific glassware and hot-surface instructions.

Consider another format if…

  • You need two independently controlled cooking zones for a main and a side.
  • You expect the PowerPod to work with glass dishes you already own.
  • You need measured evidence about crispness, noise, cleaning effort or long-term durability before buying.

Advantages and limits of the configuration

These are design trade-offs, not results from physical testing.

Reasons to consider it

  • Two supplied vessel sizes let you choose the container for the food you plan to prepare.
  • The same dedicated glassware can be used for serving and storage, with the care limits in the guide.
  • A removable heating unit changes how you arrange cooking and storage space.

Limits to account for

  • One PowerPod means the two included containers are not two independently heated zones.
  • The system depends on compatible glassware and the appropriate adapter.
  • Glass, lids, crisper plates and the heating unit have different handling and care requirements.

What the glass-container workflow changes

A drawer air fryer asks you to plan around its basket. The Crispi instead asks you to plan around a removable heating unit and the vessel beneath it. The useful buying question is whether keeping food in a dedicated cooking and serving container would simplify your routine.

For example, someone who alternates between a smaller lunch and a larger evening batch may value having two vessel sizes. Someone making two foods on separate settings has a different need: moving a heating unit between containers is not the same as running two zones together.

That is a configuration-based distinction, not a finding about speed or food quality. We have not cooked those meals with this appliance.

Capacity: compare the food layout, not a combined total

Treat the supplied vessels as alternatives. Adding their nominal volumes would give a misleading picture of how much food can cook at once. The shape and depth of your usual ingredients matter as well as the number printed on a container.

Before buying, think about a normal meal: does the main need its own setting, and would a side wait for another batch? If that would be inconvenient, compare a dual-basket layout. We do not assign a reliable serving count or usable cooking area from the advertised volume or the hero illustration.

Four modes, without an invented performance score

The documented mode list gives you a way to check whether the appliance covers the jobs you intend to do. It does not establish that one mode produces a better result than another appliance, or that a recipe written for a drawer model transfers unchanged.

The guide documents mode selection and time adjustment, rather than a separate temperature-setting control. If choosing a precise temperature is central to your recipes, compare a model that explicitly provides that control. Use the FN101CGY guide for operating instructions; cooking times and a crispness advantage have not been measured here.

Glassware and cleanup: account for the whole system

A cook-and-store concept can reduce transfers between dishes when it matches your habits. It also leaves you with multiple types of parts to manage. Dishwasher compatibility for a container is not a claim that the entire appliance can go in the dishwasher.

The owner's guide requires care with hot glass and temperature changes, and it restricts the PowerPod to the intended Ninja glassware. Read those instructions before use. Do not treat the word portable as permission to move the appliance while cooking, or use it in a moving vehicle.

We have not timed cleanup, inspected wear or tested breakage resistance. Decide whether the vessel-and-lid arrangement suits your storage space rather than assuming glass automatically means less work.

Questions before buying

Does the FN101CGY cook in both glass containers at the same time?

It has one removable PowerPod. The supplied vessels offer two size choices; they are not two independently heated cooking zones.

Can I use a glass dish I already own?

Do not assume compatibility. The manufacturer's guide restricts the PowerPod to the intended Ninja glass containers and describes the adapter for the large vessel.

Is the entire appliance dishwasher-safe?

No. The guide permits dishwasher cleaning of the glass containers but specifically excludes the PowerPod. Follow the separate instructions for each component.

How many people does it feed?

We do not promise a serving count. Consider the food layout and whether you would need separate batches; nominal container volume does not establish the size of your meal.

Has KitchenGearLab physically tested the FN101CGY?

No. This review uses the Canadian manufacturer page and the FN101CGY owner's guide, checked on November 13, 2026. The benefits and limits are editorial analysis of the documented configuration, not measured results.
